## About the Game
**FloodGame** is an educational, interactive 3D simulation game designed to teach students about flood prevention, emergency management, and community resilience. 

As the emergency manager of your city, you are given a budget to deploy engineering defenses and protective policies before a major flood event crests. Your goal is to protect as many citizens and critical buildings as possible while keeping costs within budget!

---

## How to Play

### 1. Launch the Game
Open the game website link provided by your teacher in a modern web browser (Google Chrome, Safari, or Firefox are recommended).

### 2. Select Your Location
* **Predefined Locations**: Pick one of the 6 pre-built historical maps (such as Saint Bernard Parish, LA or Des Moines, IA) to test standard scenarios.
* **Custom Location Sandbox**: Switch to the **Custom Location** tab and enter any city or town in the United States (e.g., *Seattle, WA* or *Syracuse, NY*) to generate a custom 3D map of that exact area using real-world terrain heights and street networks.

### 3. Inspect the Terrain
* Hover your cursor over any tile to see its **elevation**, **structure type**, and **risk category** in the HUD panel.
* Use the **Risk Overlay** button (red/yellow grid) to highlight low-lying, vulnerable areas likely to flood first.

### 4. Deploy Your Defenses
Click on a map tile to open the mitigation menu and select your strategy:
* **Flood Walls ($1.2M)**: High-cost, 100% waterproof concrete barriers. Best for blocking water along main riverbank channels.
* **Sandbags ($150k)**: Low-cost, temporary sandbags. Great for quick perimeter defense around single buildings.
* **Elevate Structure ($500k)**: Elevates the selected building on stilts above the expected water level.
* **Dry Floodproofing ($300k)**: Places a waterproof seal around the structure's walls to prevent water from entering.
* **Wet Floodproofing ($100k)**: Modifies the crawlspace to let water flow through safely, reducing structural pressure.
* **Flood Insurance ($300k)**: Financial protection that helps cover damages (does not stop water, but saves money post-flood).

### 5. Launch the Simulation!
Once you have placed your defenses, click the **Launch Simulation** button to watch the floodwaters crest. You can also let the countdown timer run down to zero to start the simulation automatically. Observe how your choices hold up and see if your defenses succeed in protecting the city!

## Learning Challenges & Scenarios

* **Saint Bernard Parish (Louisiana)**: Protect low-elevation coastal zones from incoming storm surges along sea canals.
* **Greenville (Mississippi)**: Defend agricultural lands and residential areas from major riverbank crevasses.
* **Des Moines (Iowa)**: Protect critical infrastructure, including the central Water Works utility, police/fire stations, and high-density downtown blocks.
* **Iowa City (Iowa)**: Shield downtown university buildings and local parks along the Iowa River.
* **Cedar Rapids (Iowa)**: Balance defensive budgets to protect heavy industrial plants and water treatment assets.
* **Davenport (Iowa)**: Defend an open riverfront without permanent barriers using temporary mobile walls and residential wet/dry floodproofing.

## Tips for Success (Using the AI Advisor)
* Use the **AI Tutor Chat panel** on the side drawer. If you get stuck, type questions like: 
  * *"What should I focus on saving?"*
  * *"How should I allocate my budget?"*
  * *"What is the difference between wet and dry floodproofing?"*
* The chat advisor will automatically analyze your current city's terrain, remaining budget, and vulnerabilities to give you customized hints!
